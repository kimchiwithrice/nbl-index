How it works

{{ rating }} follows the KenPom approach. For every game we count possessions (field-goal attempts minus offensive rebounds plus turnovers plus 0.44 × free-throw attempts) and points per 100 possessions for each side. Then every team's offense and defense, plus home-court advantage, are solved together with ridge regression. Because they're solved at once, a team that piled up points against bad defenses gets marked down, and one that held up against good offenses gets credit. Tempo (possessions per 40 minutes) is solved the same way.

Recent games count more: a game's weight halves every {{ bt.half_life if bt else 120 }} days. Last season's games count as a prior that fades as this season's games come in, so early-season ratings lean on last year. Ratings are pulled gently toward average until there's enough evidence, which stops a team that's played three games from looking like a champion.

Predictions combine the two teams' offense, defense and tempo into an expected score. The win probability comes from how big the model's misses have historically been: games swing about ±{{ cal.sigma | f1 }} points around the prediction. The season simulator plays out the remaining schedule and the finals 10,000 times. In each run every team's rating is also nudged up or down by a random amount, because the ratings are only estimates. The size of that nudge was set by replaying past seasons, and it shrinks as the season goes on.
