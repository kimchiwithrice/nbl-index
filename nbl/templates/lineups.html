What does "per 100 possessions" mean?

Most numbers here are points scored or allowed for every 100 times a team has the ball. That puts fast and slow teams on the same footing. A team scoring 115 and allowing 105 has a net rating of +10. More in the glossary.

What is on/off, and how are these built?

On/off compares how the team does while a player is on the court against how it does while they sit on the bench. "Net on" is the scoring margin per 100 possessions with them playing, "Net off" without them, and "On − off" the gap. A big positive gap suggests the team is better with them out there, though it also depends on who comes in instead.

From {{ lu.games }} of this season's games ({{ lu.min | round | int }} minutes). Lineups are rebuilt from the substitutions in the play-by-play, and a game is only used if they check out against the box score: five on court the whole way, points matching the final score and every player's minutes within a minute of the official figure. {% if deep %}This season {{ deep.games }} of {{ deep.of }} games passed.{% endif %} Lineups need 10 minutes together to be listed, pairs 20, and players need 20 minutes both on court and on the bench for on/off. Small samples still swing a lot: a lineup's net rating over 20 minutes says little on its own, and on/off depends heavily on who the backups are. About these numbers

"% of shots" is how much of the team's shooting comes from each kind of shot, and "made %" is how often those shots go in. Free throws aren't included. Shot types are as the official scorers log them; floaters and hooks are mostly from 2 to 3 metres. {% if L.zones %}Locations are logged from 2025-26 on: at the rim is within 1.5 m of the basket, short is 1.5 to 3 m, and corner 3s are taken within 3 m of the baseline.{% endif %} Includes finals.
